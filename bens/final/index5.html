How Corporations Manipulate Dopamine

The human brain, like all mammalian brains, is hard-wired to work toward things we desire. When we think about something we want or truly need, our brain releases dopamine in certain regions of the brain and on the excitatory dopamine receptors (DRD1 & DRD5) in order to motivate us for that want or need. Dopamine was a spectacular creation in prehistoric times in combination with the exceptionally advanced human brain, but due to our species’ unprecedented and astounding advancement, it’s taken a perverse turn in the modern era.

Now with the almost-incomprehensibly grotesque advent of online shopping, gambling, sex, drugs, fast food, along with anything you can immediately buy, discover or download on the internet, our brains are releasing far more dopamine than they ever have before.

Unsurprisingly, corporations have taken great advantage of dopamine, what is called “neuromarketing”. Almost no other field is as manipulative and exploitative than the fascinating realm of neuromarketing, or consumer neuroscience. Neuromarketing is essentially a combination of neuroscience, psychiatry, psychology and marketing, with the ultimate goal of predicting and even manipulating consumer behaviour.

Peer-reviewed studies from the early 2000s and even before then began to show significant connections between branding, advertising, marketing tactics and consumer decision-making. The findings showed that the brain releases large amounts of dopamine, in some cases as much or even more dopamine than what’s released during sex, in response to the various stimuli.

When the brain is promised a reward, whether status, monetary, sexual or indulgence, it immediately releases dopamine. Due to the complexity of the human brain combined with current state of our civilization, these stimuli may now propel us to do something that we may not actually desire personally. This collision of reactions in the brain causes us to do, buy, or otherwise crave something we may not have had any interest in prior.

By far the greatest example of the powerful effects of dopamine is in the online world of the internet and social media. Facebook, on multiple occasions, has even said that it is not by accident, but rather by design.

They have admitted that the social network was not actually founded in order to unite us, but to distract us, by using dopamine, because the app is designed in a way where the brain is consistently releasing dopamine at a dramatically higher rate when compared with doing mundane tasks, and because dopamine is responsible for encoding both short and long-term memory, while it’s being released at a much higher rate, these experiences become hard-wired into the brain so much so that it can feel like a necessity to use, even if it is harming you. It’s actually the exact same principle that causes drug addiction in people, except the social media form of this is different in that it’s a social-validation feedback loop, which dopamine is already heavily involved in. On top of that, due to it being involved with the psychosocial aspects of our brain, many other neurotransmitters are also released in combination with dopamine, like serotonin, endorphins, endogenous opioids, oxytocin, which makes the experience even more rewarding than dopamine on its own.

Examples abound, especially among my generation, of people checking, re checking, triple checking their social media, watching YouTube and Netflix for hours at a time without breaks, sitting in awkward and unhealthy positions, just goes to show you the power big tech corporations in particular have over our brain and particularly with respect to dopamine, and especially among adolescents, which release around three to four times as much dopamine as a fully-developed adult would in response to the same stimuli. Many studies have shown, especially among children and adolescents that in response to branding, media, social media, our brains are releasing similar amounts of dopamine as nicotine would in a developed adult would, with short bursts releasing amounts of dopamine identical compared with what’s release in the case of cocaine in a developed adult.

These levels of dopamine are high enough that they change your brains priorities, and suddenly these once unnecessary things can feel like a necessity, and things you may have prioritized a lot may not be so prioritized anymore, because there are just countless really easy and potent dopamine producers, and because these high amounts of dopamine are being produced, you’re less self aware because you temporarily feel good, and eventually this gets encoded in the brain as something necessary for survival, which to be fair, certain aspects of technology can be and are necessities for survival.

The food industry is another example of an industry which manipulates dopamine in an addictive fashion. Over time, the more dopamine release that occurs in the brain in response to that particular stimuli, the brain will temporarily shut down some dopamine receptors and sometimes other kinds receptors which make the experience less rewarding, when that experience or action happens to be a conditioned response, which is why after long-term use of certain stimuli like social media, you will over time feel less reward. The food industry knows this fact very well, which is why they are constantly changing their menus, adding new menus, new items, using coupons, etc. to keep us wanting more.

Food companies have perfected the amount of sugar, salt and fat to increase dopamine production so much that it can become an irrational craving. Food companies also use colours like red to further increase hunger, because humans have an instinct, or unconditioned response of becoming hungry and salivating in response to the sight of the colour red, which is now, at least in the western world, for mostly prehistoric reasons.
