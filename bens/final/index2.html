Introduction to Dopamine

Dopamine is both an excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitter and hormone of the phenethylamine and catecholamine families It plays a fundamental and critical role in the way the human brain works. There are six dopamine receptors in the human brain, ranging from DRD1 to DRD5, with DRD4 having the least affinity and DRD2 having the most affinity for dopamine. Dopamine is fully responsible for over 100 fundamental molecular functions and biological processes not only in the nervous system, but also much of your body. There is also the sixth TAAR1 receptor, which happens to have high affinity for dopamine, serotonin, glutamate and norepinephrine at the same time. All mammals and even many insects use dopamine in their own central nervous systems as a fundamental part of their functioning.
