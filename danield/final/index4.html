As said before, there are not just buses or SkyTrains that are the only things Translink controls. In addition with those notable modes of Public Transit, Translink also takes control of a passenger vessel that travels through the Burrard Inlet, the SeaBus. Translink even has its own commuter rail line connecting Downtown with numerous towns, the West Coast Express.

The SeaBus

The SeaBus is TransLink's passenger vessel service travelling by water along Burrard Inlet. Currently, the SeaBus has 1 line running between 2 docks, 1 dock stationed at Waterfront to connect with the SkyTrain, WCE, Buses and Downtown Vancouver. The other dock is stationed at Lonsdale Quay, connecting the North Shore with buses to North Vancouver and West Vancouver.

There actually is a lot of history that goes a far way back even before Vancouver's name was established. The first known (unofficial) ferry service in Burrard Inlet was in 1866. Official ferry service commenced by the start of the 1900s. The ferry service continued until the '30s when the Lions Gate Bridge was built, reducing demand drastically. The demand increased again when the Second World War occured, which needed labourers to travel between Burrard Inlet to work on new resources and equipment to help in the war effort, such as constructing many Ships in the Shipyards Docks. Once the 1950s came around after World War 2, demand was decreasing once again which eventually discontinued the ferry service.

Later on, the Government worked with Vancouver's Transit system to gain more knowledge on how to properly run an operational ferry service in the Burrard Inlet. They plan out the new passenger vessel line which commenced service in 1977, under ownership of BC Hydro, the owner of Vancouver's transit system at the time. The SeaBus opened with 2 vessels ready to go, the Burrard Otter and Burrard Beaver. These vessels were first painted in pure red-orange, to stand out while travelling across the Burrard Inlet for larger cargo ships to spot easily from afar. The service continued to thrive up until today, with 3 new vessels added along the way, replacing the Burrard Otter as it aged. The new additions were:

  • The Burrard Pacific Breeze, added to the fleet in 2009 to help keep a vessel as a spare in case another one wasn't fit for service
  • The Burrard Otter II, added to the fleet in 2014 to add extra service and eventually deemed the replacement for the Burrard Otter
  • The Burrard Chinook, commenced service in 2021 to bring more frequent service on the SeaBus

The West Coast Express

The West Coast Express, known abbreviatively as the WCE, is TransLink's commuter rail service bringing commuters to and from their daily work routine in Downtown Vancouver. Currently, the West Coast Express connects Downtown Vancouver with the Tri-Cities, Pitt Meadows, Maple Ridge, all the way to Mission, adjacent north of Abbotsford.

Planning started in the '70s when Executives came up with an idea to have fast, efficient rail service using existing railways such as the CPR or the BNSF. This came back up in 1993 where Executives decided where the new commuter line should be.

On November 1, 1995, the new commuter line opened for the public to travel towards Downtown Vancouver in the morning, and travel from Downtown Vancouver in the Afternoon or Evening. The stations served by the West Coast Express, listed from west to east, are:

  • Waterfront Station
  • Port Moody Station
  • Coquitlam Central Station
  • Port Coquitlam Station
  • Pitt Meadows Station
  • Maple Meadows Station
  • Port Haney Station
  • Mission Station

Now, a more in-depth explanation of the West Coast Express Schedule:

The West Coast Express is designed to transport commuters to and from Downtown Vancouver every weekday, solely for work purposes. This means that the West Coast Express is a commuter service that has inbound-only trains picking up commuters from other towns to transport them to Downtown Vancouver's Waterfront Station in the morning. Then, when the usual work period ends in the afternoon/evening, trains pick commuters up at Waterfront Station for them to be transported back out of Downtown. With this, service only happens in peak times, or rush-hour, with no service mid-day, at night, or on the weekend.
