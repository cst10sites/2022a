The SkyTrain is the Light Rail Rapid Transit Network, but it may not be just that to some. Some people consider it a distinguishable component of pointing out Metro Vancouver, as the fully automatic, driverless system is so unique.

Although each part of the SkyTrain will be elaborated on, here is some general info on the SkyTrain:

The SkyTrain first opened to the public on December 11, 1985 (Under ownership of BC Transit since Translink wasn't existent just yet), just in time for Vancouver's World Fair: Expo 86 (Hence the name of one of today's lines, the Expo Line). It was perfect timing as well since the theme for the 1986 World Fair was based on Transportation. This gave BC Transit the opportunity to showcase to the world one of the very first automatic rail lines, and would be of course one of the longest in a very long time. Today, the SkyTrain consists of 3 lines: The Expo Line, Millennium Line, and Canada Line, each covering a different part of Metro Vancouver

The current fleet consists of 150 Mark I cars, 108 Mark II cars, and 84 Mark III cars, with more sets along the way within the next few years (Mark V).

The Expo Line: How it all started

Planning and Opening

As said before, the Expo Line was pretty much the start of the SkyTrain. However, it didn't get its name until the opening of the Millennium Line, since after all it was only 1 line back in the day.

Now, you may wonder how the planning came along for a new, innovative rapid transit system to come into play to serve Metro Vancouver. Up until the 1950s where it closed down, there actually used to be an interurban streetcar line running by where the SkyTrain is today. Along with other lines, this line transported passengers from Downtown Vancouver all the way out into the Fraser Valley. However, with the rise of bus service taking over ridership, it was decided that the Interurban line had to be shut down.

The next decade or two saw normal service, but when ridership continued to increase and traffic congestion started to clog many roads, Vancouver needed to come up with a faster, more reliable system to meet the growing demands and reduce traffic congestion. There were plans of having a freeway coming right into the Downtown area, but it was opposed since lots of demolition would be involved, wiping out much of Chinatown and Gastown.

With freeways off the table, Metro Vancouver decided to choose Rapid Transit as a solution, specifically for a "light rail" network.

At the same time, UTDC, the Urban Transport Development Corporation, was testing a newly innovative driverless train system at their headquarters. This was called the Intermediate Capacity Transit System (ICTS). It's smaller than a normal subway, yet more cost-effective and capable of holding the same capacity, if not more.

As BC Transit talked with UTDC, they settle on a deal and sign contracts in 1981 for Vancouver to be the first city to receive such a technologically advanced rapid transit system, with constructing a line from the Downtown Waterfront to New Westminister, served by 114 UTDC ICTS cars in 57 2-car sets.

To demonstrate to Vancouverites on how the system will work, a kilometre-long track line was built along Terminal Ave, along with Main Street Station (Now Main Street-Science World). This was completed and opened for citizens to observe mid-1983 using some of UTDC's test vehicles from Ontario.

While the demonstration was open, construction continued on for the rest of the line to Waterfront and New Westminister for the next couple years. As the demonstration completed, the test cars were sent back to the UTDC headquarters, and the actual ICTS cars were being delivered. As everything was set, the unofficial opening was on December 11, 1985. The official, public, full-service opening was in the first few days of 1986, in time for the '86 World's Fair on Transportation. Along with gondolas and monorails spanning across False Creek, guests from around the world were very interesed and fascinated with the SkyTrain, which would be the future of Light Rail Transportation.

Extensions

Soon, interest grew, and an extension was suggested. With this, BC Transit planned for the SkyTrain to be extended to Surrey. Phase 1 opened with the SkyTrain getting another station in New West (Columbia) in 1989, while the SkyTrain Bridge over the Fraser River was being constructed. Come 1990, Scott Road Station was opened, the first station in Surrey. In 1994, 3 more stations (Gateway, Surrey Central, and King George) were added. With this extension, BC Transit purchased for 36 new Mark I cars, 16 of which were meant to be sent to Milan, Italy for a train line there, but was then cancelled. With Milan's different configuration requests before cancellation, this is why you'd see some Mark Is with no front doors and instead a bigger window.

When the Millennium Line opened in 2002, this was when the original SkyTrain line got its name as the "Expo Line".

Now, there are plans for another extension of the Expo Line. Executives call for an extension to the city of Langley, bringing new service from the existing King George Station to several new stations along the Fraser Highway over to Langley. Construction has not started yet for the extension, and this is predicted to open by 2028.

The line continues to thrive today, with the same original line running from Waterfront to King George, and a new branch commencing service in Fall 2016 (in lieu of moving Millennium Line operations), running the same line from Waterfront to Columbia, then splitting off to run through Coquitlam to Lougheed and Production Way. This line is served by Mark I cars, newer-generation Mark II cars, Mark III cars, and occasionally older-generation Mark II cars.

The Millennium Line

Planning and Opening

The Millennium is the second line of the SkyTrain to open, first opened in 2002. Where it got its name is almost self-explanatory: The line was constructed during the new millennium year 2000.

The line was planned in the mid-to-late '90s after a new plan to extend the SkyTrain network along Lougheed Highway to loop around and terminate at a new platform area at Broadway Station, now known as Commercial-Broadway for the two lines coming together on seperate platforms. Phase 1 of the Millennium line opened in 2002 with the route running from Waterfront to Braid Stations. Phase 2 opened later that year extending the Braid Terminus all the way to Broadway Station. It was not until 2006 where VCC-Clark Station was opened, which is now the current western terminus of the Millennium Line.

With an entirely new line opening and Translink now in ownership of the SkyTrain, they needed to order a new set of trains. This would be called the Mark II, the evolution to the ICTS Mark I. 60 cars in 30 sets were ordered. Although each of the cars were longer, they had an accordion in the middle which allowed passengers to travel in between cars without having to exit and enter the train again, unlike the Mark Is.

With new ownership, the Mark 2 trains received a new paint livery which consisted of white, blue and yellow, instead of BC Transit's white, blue and red paint.

Extensions

Later on in the early 2010s, a SkyTrain line to Coquitlam was called for. The "Evergreen Extension" would add 6 new stations to the Millennium Line to serve the Tri-Cities, up to Lafarge Lake and Douglas College, the current Eastern Terminus of the line. Adding this extension reformed the SkyTrain Lines, which no longer had the Millennium Line travel to Waterfront come Fall of 2016. The Evergreen Extension opened to the public on December 2nd, 2016, bringing new rapid transit service to Coquitlam.

A future extension that you might've heard about is the Broadway Extension. This is an under-construction extension of the Millennium Line west of VCC-Clark Station. This will add 6 new stations along Broadway in Vancouver up to Arbutus Street. These stations will follow where the current 99 B-Line runs now, replacing that part of the route. The extension is predicted to open in 2025.

Today, the Millennium Line runs from VCC-Clark and Lafarge Lake-Douglas, served by older-generation Mark II sets.

The Canada Line

Planning and Opening

The Canada Line is the third line of the SkyTrain. The line opened in 2009 just months before Vancouver would host the 2010 Olympics, which came up with the name: Canada, as a sign of our own country representing the Olympics.

Planning dated back to the '90s where Metro Vancouver wanted a Rapid Transit line connecting Vancouver and Richmond. This was first established with bus service between the two cities: the 98 B-Line. This would run via Number 3 road, over to Sea Island to connect bus service with YVR Airport, going along Granville into Downtown, and Vice Versa.

Around the same time, Translink continued to discuss of plans for a better, more effective Rapid Transit System from Vancouver to Richmond. They chose the option of a SkyTrain route along Cambie rather than a light rail line along Arbutus, which commenced construction in 2005.

The brand-new line opened on August 17, 2009, to bring service along Richmond Centre, directly to YVR Airport, along Vancouver's Cambie Corridor and Downtown. This is the same route that you see today: Two Canada Line branches running from Waterfront to either Richmond-Brighouse or YVR-Airport. Today, the Canada Line operates 64 cars in 32 sets: 20 sets in service by the opening, and an additional 12 sets in service between 2019-2020, to bring more frequent service.

Extensions (?)

As of right now, there aren't any extensions planned for. The only addition the Canada Line will have is a new station in Richmond between Bridgeport and Aberdeen Stations: Capstan Station at Capstan way and Number 3 road. Construction will be starting come 2022 and is slated to be open within a year or two after the start of construction.
