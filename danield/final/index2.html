Buses are the workhorse of the Transit System. Carrying hundreds of thousands of riders every day, it takes lots of responsibility to ensure Translink keeps its reputation. Across the system, there are over 200 bus routes, which are run by over a thousand vehicles out on the roads every day. All other buses are kept and maintained in the transit yards by mechanics, ensuring their performance runs smoothly for a positive customer experience.

Bus service is run by a subsidiary of Translink: Coast Mountain Bus Company. It is the bus subsidiary of Translink, just like how the train subsidiary is the SkyTrain/BCRTC.

Descriptions of Bus Services

Standard

Translink's standard bus service is exactly what it means; the standard, normal bus service of Metro Vancouver. All routes within this category will have a somewhat unique frequency running either all 7 days of the week, weekdays only, weekends only, or only certain parts of the year, such as routes that travel to a beach (routes like the 42, 150, 179 operate Summer only since they travel to Spanish Banks, White Pine Beach and Buntzen Lake respectively.). Depending on the ridership of each route, some routes may have buses that come by as fast as every 5 minutes, while other routes with lower ridership may have a frequency as low as 1 bus for every hour, which only would be the case for routes in fairly remote areas and some other routes on the weekend.

As standard bus routes take up the majority of Translink's bus service, you may find all sorts of different buses depending on the route's area. Your next trip could be a standard diesel bus, a diesel-electric hybrid, a CNG vehicle, an articulated bus, or even a fully electric trolleybus or battery bus, which will be talked about next...

B-Line and RapidBus

The B-Line and RapidBus are part TransLink's rapid transit network, specifically for buses. These routes offer high-quality express service which is guaranteed 10-minute-or-faster service all day, and serves from dawn to late at night.

You may have heard that the B-Line is being replaced by RapidBus, which is almost fully true. Although RapidBus is the official evolution of the B-Line, there is still 1 B-Line route remaining, the 99. This is because of how the Broadway SkyTrain Extension is already underway and it would be unnecessary to bring all the newer equipment to the 99. However, there were in fact 4 other B-Line routes that were running. The following list shows the list of all B-Line routes, alive or dead, and how/why they are (being) replaced:

  • 95 B-Line - Started in 2016 to service Hastings St - Rebranded by R5 RapidBus in 2020
  • 96 B-Line - Started in 2013 to service King George Boulevard and 104 Ave in Surrey - Rebranded by R1 RapidBus in 2020
  • 97 B-Line - Started in 2002 to service Lougheed and Coquitlam Central - Replaced by the Millennium Line Evergreen Extension in 2016
  • 98 B-Line - Started in 2000 to service Downtown to Richmond - Replaced by the Canada Line in 2009
  • 99 B-Line - Originally started in the '90s for fast service between Lougheed and UBC - Will be partially replaced by the Broadway Extension east of Arbutus in 2025

Now, onto RapidBus: RapidBus is Translink's current bus rapid transit branding, which, as said before, is the "evolution" of the B-Line. The buses used for RapidBus service have a green paint livery and are articulated hybrid buses. In technical terms to others, the New Flyer XDE60 is used for RapidBus service.

The RapidBus currently consists of 5 routes, each starting with the initial 'R':

  • R1 King George RapidBus (Newton Exchange - Guildford) - Started in January 2020, rebranding the 96 B-Line
  • R2 Marine Drive RapidBus (Park Royal - Phibbs Exchange) - Started later in April 2020, bringing new express service along North Vancouver
  • R3 Lougheed Hwy RapidBus (Coquitlam Central - Haney Place) - Started in January 2020, bringing new express service between Coquitlam and Maple Ridge.
  • R4 41st Ave RapidBus (UBC - Joyce Station) - Started in January 2020, replacing the 43 to bring all-day every day express service along 41st Ave in Vancouver.
  • R5 Hastings Street RapidBus (Burrard Station - SFU) - Started in January 2020, rebranding the 95 B-Line

Additionally, there are already more RapidBus routes planned for, including the R6 Scott Road RapidBus which would run between Scott Road Station and Newton Exchange in Surrey, commencing service in 2023.

Community Shuttles

Community Shuttles are pretty much the mini buses of TransLink. They consist of smaller shuttle vehicles carrying up to 20 people for them to be run on shorter, less demanding routes.

Many of the Community Shuttle routes are run by one depot: Hamilton Transit Center. Hamilton Transit Center runs all community shuttles as far as Delta, UBC, Surrey, and Burnaby, and of course including the areas closer. Other Community Shuttle routes are run by Port Coquitlam Transit Center, and West Vancouver Municipal Transit, which is contracted to West Vancouver Blue Bus.

Emission-reducing Bus Types

Hybrid and CNG Buses

Along with electric buses, TransLink has other buses that also have a reduced amount of emissions to bring their carbon footprint down even further. With this, routes are served with hundreds of Diesel-Electric Hybrid buses and buses that use CNG, the acronym for Compressed Natural Gas.

You'd wonder how TransLink (CMBC) would gather its resources in order to fuel up these vehicles. While Diesel-Electric Hybrids are filled up with regular diesel, CNGs are filled up with renewable natural gas provided by FortisBC, whom is under contract with Translink for the next few years.

Other Information

Transit Centers

You may have heard about the term "Transit Center" before. In other places, that name may be referred to as a somewhat large transit exchange. For TransLink, the term refers to the depots for where vehicle buses are stored when not in use, typically overnight. All vehicles are assigned to a depot, mainly depending on their model and the equipment they have. Since TransLink covers the entire Metro Vancouver Area, they would need multiple depots spread across the region. With this, here is a list of all Transit Centers and the areas they cover:

  • Vancouver Transit Center (VTC) - Covers the City of Vancouver specific parts of Burnaby. Also where newly hired operators are trained.
  • Burnaby Transit Center (BTC) - Covers North Vancouver, Burnaby, and some parts of Vancouver
  • Richmond Transit Center (RTC) - Covers Richmond, Delta/North Delta, and parts of White Rock.
  • Hamilton Transit Center (HTC) - Covers Burnaby, New West, and some parts of the Tri-Cities and Surrey. Also covers Community Shuttle routes in the western area of Metro Vancouver.
  • Surrey Transit Center (STC) - Covers Surrey, White Rock, Delta, and Langley.
  • Port Coquitlam Transit Center (PTC) - Covers the Tri-Cities, and areas above the Tri-Cities like Belcarra and Anmore. Also covers Community Shuttle routes in these areas.
  • West Vancouver Transit Center (WVMT) - Contracted to Translink with West Vancouver to serve the town with West Vancouver "Blue Buses"

Want to know how to find out what depot a bus belongs to? You may have seen a "fleet number" posted on the front, sides, or back of a bus. This is a 4-5 digit number that helps keep track of which bus is where. You may have also found a letter in front of that number, which is the indicator for what depot it belongs to. However, sometimes this may not be the case if a bus was recently transferred to another depot, where the letter wasn't changed right away.

Here are a list of letters that belong to each depot:

  • V - (VTC)
  • B - (BTC)
  • R - (RTC)
  • H - (HTC)
  • S - (STC)
  • P - (PTC)
  • T - Vehicles used for training only

In some cases, like trolleybuses, vehicles might also not have any letter, due to their limitations that they cannot be transferred to another depot.
