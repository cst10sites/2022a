A Forgotten Technology

Introduction

8-Track Tapes, or more formally Stereo 8, was a continous audio tape catridge format, popular fromthe late 60s to the early 80s. It was developed by Bill Lear's Jet Corporation in 1963. They were primarily popular in the automobile industry and to a lesser degree in the home market. Pre-recorded tapes were avalible, as well as blank tapes, although they were as popular. The tapes were continous, drawing and winding on to one, free spinning reel. Tape was driven by a capstan and consisted of four stereo programs, totaling eight stereo tracks giving it is name. The 8-track eventually lost popularily to the compact cassette in the early 80s due to its many techincal flaws.
