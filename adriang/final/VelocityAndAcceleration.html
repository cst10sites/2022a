Velocity and acceleration

       To first understand what is velocity, we must first comprehend motion. Motion is everything around us that is moving, planes flying, people jogging, or apples falling on Isaac Newton's leading him to come up with the concept of gravity. We can call that speed and it can be expressed as distance over time. For example, if a car drives 50 kilometers in one hour, we can say that that car has a speed of 50 kilmoeters per hour (or if you do the math, 13.8888888... meters per second). Velocity is a combination of speed and direction and is defined as the rate of change in position with a respect to a frame of reference. For example, if a car goes 50 kilometers in an hour and turns around and goes back to its starting location in an hour, we would say that that car has a speed of 50 kilometers per hour but a velocity of zero. Since the object had a net change of zero meters over two hours, it has a velocity of zero.


       Acceleration is defined as the change in velocity or a=ΔV/t, a is acceleration, ΔV is the change in velocity, and t is the time. For example, if Dingo goes from 0 to 50 km per hour in 5 seconds...

a=(final velocity-initial velocity)/elapsed time=50 km per hour/5 seconds=50km per hour/5 seconds×(1 Hour/3600 seconds) x (1000 meters/1 km)= 2.78meters/s2


       Please Note the following: 1)Note that the units of acceleration are m/s2, meters per second per second. 2)The factors (1 Hour/3600 seconds) and (1000 meters/1 km) both equal to 1 and we introduce them to convert hours to seconds and meters to kilometers. 3)When an object's velocity slows down, we say it decelerates. 4)For beginner physics, we usually stick to examples of constant acceleration not fluctuating acceleration. 5)Velocity and acceleration are vector quantities which means that it has a direction as well as a magnitude.

       Imagine that this golf ball is a car and Dingo is driving the car around a circular track at a constant speed of 20 km/h. Although his speed is constant, his velocity is not. His velocity (in the picture it is described with the symbol V) is whatever direction the front of his car is pointing towards, and thus the it is constantly changing as he goes around the circle. The acceleration is perpendicular to the velocity and points to the center of the circle (acceleration is described using the symbol aC. In conclusion, when an object moves in a circular motion, with constant speed, its velocity is constantly changing and its acceleration is towards the center of the circle.

       Suppose you start from rest and undergo constant acceleration "a" for a period of time "t". How far do you go in this time?


       So firstly, since you're at rest at the start, your initial speed is zero. And since you accelerate uniformly, your final velocity is equal to a times t. Therefore your average velocity during the time interval was: vaverage=(0+at)/2=½at. If you rearrange the equation for velocity v=d/t to find distance, you get d=vt. And since we know that v=1/2at, we can plug it in to get...


d=½at×t=½at2


       For example, suppose that we use the previous example that Dingo accelerates from 0 to 50 km/h in 5 seconds. We can use the equation d=½at×t=½at2, we already found that the acceleration is equal to 2.78m/sec2 and we know that the time is equal to 5 seconds. All that is left is to plug all that information into our equation to get an answer.


d=½at2=½(2.78m/s2)×(5 s)2=34.7 meters


       If you were on the moon and you dropped a brick and a feather from the same height and at the exact same moment, which one would make it to the ground first? It may seem counter intuitive but they would both hit the ground at the same time. All objects fall with the same acceleration, regardless of mass. A feather just has a lot of air resistance as opposed to the brick which has less air resistance. But in a vacuum, such as the moon, the feather drops like a brick and ultimately they both fall at the same speed. From experimentation, we have determined that the rate of acceleration of an object near the surface of the Earth is 32ft/sec2=9.8m/sec2 (neglecting air resistance and it is written using the variable "g"). Einstein reasoned that because all objects move the same in a gravitational field, gravity must be a property of space and time rather than of the objects themselves (but that is a story for another time).


       Let's say we drop a block of concrete from a rooftop. It's acceleration is equal to g, 9.8m/s2, thus the velocity of the falling brick is v=gt. After one second of falling, it is going (9.8m/s2)x(1s)=9.8m/s. After two seconds of falling, the speed/velocity is (9.8m/s2)x(2 s)=19.6m/s, etc.


       How far does it fall in time "t"? Apply our formula d=½dt2. After one second, it has fallen ½(9.8m/s2)×(1s)2=4.9 meters. After two seconds, the distance is ½(9.8m/s2)(2s)2=19.6 meters.
