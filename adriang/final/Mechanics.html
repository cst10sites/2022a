Potential energy

       Potential energy is expressed in the formula P.E.=mgh, where P.E. is the potential energy, m is the mass in kilogrammes, g is the gravitational force (near Earth it is equal to 9.8 m/s2), h is the height in meters.

              Potential energy is the energy with the potential to do work. For example, if a massive coconut with a mass of 1.5kg is suspended 11 meters above the ground, that cocnut has the potential fall and move and when that time happens, the gravitational potential energy will be converted into kinetic energy. Calculuate the coconut's potential energy. P.E.=mgh, P.E.=(1.5kg)(9.8 m/2)(11m), P.E.=161.7 Newtons.

Kinetic energy

              Kinetic energy is expressed in the formula K.E.=½mv2, where K.E. is the kinetic energy, m is the mass in kilogrammes and v is the velocity in meters per second.

              Kinetic energy is the energy of motion. Ex. find the kinetic energy of an object with a mass of 6 kg going at 7m/s? K.E.=½mv2, K.E.=½(6kg)(7m/s)2, K.E.=½(294), K.E.=147 Newtons.

Conservation of energy(also known as the first law of thermodynamics)

              The law or conservation of energy states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed, the total energy in a system remains constant. It instead can change forms. In equation form it is written as ΔU=Q-W, where ΔU is the change in the internal energy of the closed system, Q is the quantity of energy supplied to the system as heat, and W is the amount of thermodynamic work done by the system on its surroundings.


              Let's say you fall from a height above the ground, while you are in that position, you have gravitational potential energy. While you are falling that energy is converted into kinetic energy. When you hit the ground, that kinetic energy is transfered into thermal energy. But you might be asking yourself, where did the energy it that got you above the ground come from? Well since you most likely climbed into that location, you needed kinetic energy to get up there. That took chemical potential energy from the food and beverages you consumed. That chemical energy came from photosynthesis and light energy from the sun (if you ate meat, that animal ate plants to live so this also applies). That light energy comes from the nuclear energy of hydrogen atoms fusing together in a nuclear fusion reaction.

              If you think about it, you would find that when an object is falling, the original gravitational potential energy is equal to the object's final kinetic energy, just before hitting the ground. So if we solve for v in ½mv2=mgh, we find that v is the square root of (2gh).


              Think about one of the most iconic and reckognizable symbols of physics, a Newton's Cradle. This is an object that has balls on strings attached to it and when one ball hits a line of them, one on the other side of the group pops right off and goes in the same direction. Eventually gravity slows it down and then it reverse direstion and hits the line of metal balls to restart the process all ovre again and this will continue (friction bewtween the balls and the strings plus the air will eventually ground it to a halt). But you may ask yourself "If I release one ball to hit the line of balls, why can't two balls come out the other way with each having half the speed of the original ball?" Well let's test that hypothesis into our equation for kinetic energy K.E.=½mv2+½mv2 (we add ½mv2 with itself because there are two balls that are moving. K.E.=½m(½v)2+½m(½v)2=¼mv2≠½mv2. As we can see the two are not equal and so this disproves the original hypothesis.

Conservation of momentum and impulse

              Just like energy, momentum is conserved. The total momentum in a system will remain zero. In equation form, we say that po=pf (this only remains true if there is no external force/impulse).

              Momentum is calculated as velocity times mass or in equation form it is written as . So for an object with a relatively small mass like a baseball to have the same momentum as a relatively massive object such as a truck, it would have to have a much larger relative speed than the truck. Impulse is an external force on a system over a period of time. In equation form it is written as J=FΔt, F is for force, Δt is for the time interval in which the force was applied.

              Here is a problem: let's say that there is a an apple with a mass of 0.7kg barely hanging from a tree that essentially is about to fall but you have an orange with a mass of 0.4kg. You throw the orange at the apple with a velocity of 5m/s, and then after the collision the apple goes in that direction with a velocity of 3m/s. Calculuate the velocity (and direction) of the orange after the collision (let's say that gravity is non existant or that this interaction occurs over an infinitely small period of time do that no external impulse including gravity could alter the course of this interaction).

              Since there is no impulse on the system, Jext=0. Also, since momentum is conserved, the total momentum of the system before and after the interaction remains the same. So, the initial momentum of the orange is (0.4kg)(5m/s) and the initial momentum for the apple is (0.7kg)(0m/s)=0. Let vf be the final velocity of the orange, which we are trying to find (so the final momentum of the orange is (0.4kg)(vf). The final momentum of the apple is (0.7kg)(3m/s). Therefore, (0.4kg)(5m/s)=(0.4kg)(vf)+(0.7kg)(3m/s) (the original momentum of the apple is not written because it equals 0). If we solve we get, (2kg)×m/s=(0.4kg)vf+(2.1kg×m/s). If we continue to solve we get: -0.1kg×m/s=(0.4kg)vf, then -0.25m/s=vf. The reason why the velocity is negative is that it is indicating that it is going in the opposite direction (typically, a positive velocity indicates a velocity going to the right and a negative velocity indicates a velocity going to the left). This is why if you're in outerspace with an object and you want to get somewhere, throw that object in the opposite direction of that location.

Work

              The work you apply on something is equal to the force times the distance. In other words, W=fd. Since the force(f) is equal to mass times acceleration(ma), we can deduce that work is equal to mass times acceleration times distance which gives us the equation W=mad. It is easy to remember because you can think that work makes you mad.

              Since work is the force time the distance, the units of measure of work is Newtons per meter or Nm which we call Joules. So if you move a stationary 10 kg object 2 meters with a force of 20N, we discard the mass of the object and multiply the force by the distance and get 40 Joules.

Weight

              As you may know by now, weight≠mass, but they do share some similarities. Weight is equal to mass times the gravitational force(for objects near the surface of the Earth g is equal to 9.8). In an equation, that ia written as W=mg.


              Weight is measured in Newtons, while mass is measure in kilogrammes. For example, if you have a 1 kg object on Earth, its weight is 9.8 Newtons (because 1 times 9.8 which is 9.8), if it weighs 5kg then it weighs 49 Newtons (because 5 times 9.8 equals 49), etc. To help you understand, you will always have mass no matter where in this universe you are. But in a gravitational field, you will have weight and without a gravitational field your weight is zero. The weight depends on whether a force is acting on you or not.
