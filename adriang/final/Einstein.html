Albert Einstein(14 March 1879 – 18 April 1955)


       Albert Einstein revolutionized the way we think about time, space, matter, light and more. In 1905, when he was only 26, he had his "Annus mirabilis" or miracle year in which he published not 1, not 2, not 3 but 4 consequencial papers. At that point in his life he was a PhD student working in a patent office in Bern, Switzerland and if you told any of the world's leading physicists that he would become the single most recognizable face of science ever, they simply wouldn't believe you. His four 1905 papers were about:

  1. The photoelectric effect: When a metal is being bombarded by photons, the photons "rip" the electron out of its orbit and then an electric current forms(leftover energy from that process becomes the energy of the current. At that point in history, this effect stumped even the greatest of physicists because it would contradict the idea that light is only electromagnetic radiation in the form of waves. What Einstein did was he that showed that the effect could be explained if light was a quanta of energy (a photon) and came up with a series of equations to explain its behavior. This proved that light is both a wave and a particle and Einstein won the 1921 Nobel prize in Physics for this paper.
  2. Brownian motion: At that point in time, many physicists did not accept the existance of atoms and many others believed in the idea of "invisible" atoms. Einstein showed that the random motion of particles in a liquid, also called Brownian motion, could be explained by the existance of atoms that were colliding with one another. Experiments soon confirmed Einstein's model, and his critics threw in the towel.
  3. Special relativity: For a long time, physicists could not resolve a major inconsistency in physics. The principle of relativity, going all the way back to Galileo, stated that absolute motion cannot not be defined. But electromagnetic theory asserted that absolute motion did exist. Absolute motion is the fact that the speed of light (commonly written with the symbol "c") is constant. Einstein solved the anomally by seeing that the speed of light remained constant regardless of reference frame, while both time and space were relative to the observer. This became known as Special Relativity.
  4. As a follow up to the third paper, he noticed that his theory implied that mass and energy were equivalent (despite the fact that one is solid and the other ethereal). This could be expressed in the now-famous equation E=mc2.
