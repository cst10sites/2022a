Newton's first, second and third laws

Newton's first law:

       Newton's first law states that an object at rest tends to stay at rest and an object in motion tends to stay in motion.

       Let's say that there was nothing in the universe except yourself. In that scenario, you would have no external forces (gravitational or other) being exerted on you. If you started out in that universe stationary, then you shall remain stationary for the rest of eternity. If you started out in that universe in motion you shall remain in motion for the rest of eternity. On top of that you're velocity shall remain the same (ex. if you started moving at 10 meters per second, you sahll remain in motion at a rate of 10 meters per second for the rest of eternity. All of this is based on the assumtion that there is litterally nothing in the universe except yourself, not even gas molecules such as hydrogen, because if there was and they were smashing into you as you went along, you would sooner or later ground to a halt.

Newton's second law:

       Newton's second law states that the force you exert on an object is proportional to the mass and acceleration of that object. In equation form we say that F=ma.

       Newton's second law introduced the concept of inertia, an object's resistance to changes in its motion (also known as mass). Object's with a higher mass have a higher inertia, for example your average monster truck has a larger inertia than your average butterfly. When we are talking about objects at rest, the the truck will be harder to set into motion, but ounce it is in motion it is harder to stop.

Newton's third law:

       Newton's third law states that an object will exert a force equal to that that is being exerted on it in the opposite direction.

       Let's say that you put a mug on a table. The mug will exert a gravitational force downwards, towards the Earth, that is 9.8 times the mass of the mug(let's assume that you are near the surface of the Earth). The table will exert an equal force upward to the mug to balance all the forces out. If it the table was unable to exert an equal force onto the mug, and it fell short, the table would break and collapse. If it exerted a force greater than that of the downward gravitational force, the mug and the table would go upward and levitate/fly for a few seconds.

The universal law of gravitation

       The universal law of gravitation is written as Fg=(Gm1m2)/r2. It is the law that can calculate the gravitional attraction between two masses.

       Fg is the gravitational force. G is a constant that is equal to 6.674×10−11 m3kg−1s−2, if you didn't already notice, the gravitational constant is tiny and out of the 4 fundamental forces of nature, it is by far the smallest. m1 and m2 are in the masses of the two objects in kilogrammes. r is the distance between the objects, but be careful. R is calculated as the distance between the centers of mass of the two objects, not the "direct" distance between them. So if you're physically on Earth, r is the distance between you and the center of Earth which is roughly 6 378.1 kilometers. We've found that for objects close to Earth have a gravitational force of 9.8 m/s2 so we use that to keep things simple.
