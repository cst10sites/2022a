Rules of Sabre

1. Target Area

The general target area for sabre fencing contains the entire torso above the waist, the head, and the arms up to the wrist of which a valid hit may be scored. The legs, hands and feet are excluded from the target area.

A single circuit for the entire target area used in scoring systems is formed by multiple conductive equipment: glove, Lamé, Mask.



2. Scoring Box

Scoring box notify the judge to stop the action and assist him with determining valid hits. There are four main lights which show the location of a hit on a fencer. The white lights illuminate when an invalid or off-target hit is made. The red and green lights are the scoring lights. When the green light illuminates, it means that the fencer on the judge’s left hand side has made a valid, on-target hit. Ther red light means that the fencer to the judge’s right made the valid hit.

3. Scoring

Basic Ways to Score

· Touches scored: by thrusting and cutting.

· One light action: When the scoring box only illuminates one scoring light, the fencer on the same side gets the point.

Special rule

· Right of Way: If both fencers hit each other at the same time (the scoring box illuminates two scoringb lights), the fencer with the initiative (the one who is going forward OR makes contact with the blade) gets the point.

Complex Ways to score

· Counterattack: A denfensive action, a fencer that is denfending (doesn't have the right of way) but hits the attacking fencing with a quick action, and there is only one light on the box, he gets the point.

· Parry-Riposte: When one fencer initials an attack, the other fencer that blocks his sword and hits back, gets the point, this is a parry-riposte. (The video below is about parry-roposte)
